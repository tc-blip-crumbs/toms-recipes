# Tom's Recipes house rules, version 2

## 1. Every recipe has the same parts, in this order
1. Title, in UK spelling, with "and" in place of "&".
2. One sentence saying what the dish is.
3. A line of facts. Serves, preparation time, cooking time, oven temperature if the oven is used.
4. Labels from a fixed list, such as Batch cook, Freezes well, Ted can share, Weeknight, Weekend.
5. Equipment, only when the recipe needs something beyond pans and a baking tray, such as a slow cooker or a 23 x 33cm tin.
6. Ingredients, in the order you use them. Recipes with separate parts get group headings such as "For the beef" and "For the beans".
7. Method, as numbered steps. Each step shows the ingredients it uses.
8. Notes. Storing and freezing, making ahead, the oven or grill method for air fryer recipes, and whether Ted can share it. Ted's recipes keep practical food-safety notes, such as reheating rice once, and leave out salt reminders.
9. Source, at the foot of the page. When the original has a web address, the source name links to it.

## 2. Measurements
- Solids in g and kg. Liquids in ml and litres. Switch to kg or litres at 1,000 and above, such as 1.2kg.
- Small amounts in tsp and tbsp, written as "1 tsp" and "2 tbsp". Fractions appear as ¼, ½ and ¾, never as decimals.
- Whole things as counts, with a size where it matters, such as "1 large onion" or "2 garlic cloves".
- Tins and jars as "1 x 400g tin chopped tomatoes".
- No cups, ounces, pounds, sticks or °F anywhere.
- The air fryer comes first. Whenever a dish works in an air fryer, the method uses it, such as "Heat the air fryer to 200°C", and the oven or grill version goes in a note headed "In the Oven" or "Under the Grill". Braises, bakes in a tin and whole roasts keep the oven. Air fryer steps are written for Tom's Salter DuoWave 26L, which preheats and cooks on a crisper tray or baking pan, so steps say "on the crisper tray" and "turn halfway" and never mention a basket.
- Every oven temperature is for a fan oven, such as "Heat the oven to 160°C". A recipe from a source written for a conventional oven drops by 20°C.
- Hob heat as low, medium-low, medium, medium-high or high.
- Times as "10 minutes" and "1 hour 30 minutes". Sizes in cm.
- Vague amounts are allowed only from a fixed list, such as a pinch, a handful, a squeeze and "to taste".

## 3. Words
- Subheadings use title case and "&" in place of "and", such as "Breakfast & Drinks", "For the Butter Beans" and "Good Additions". This covers course names, ingredient group headings, note headings and page labels. Short words such as "the", "for", "of" and "to" stay lower case unless they come first. Recipe titles, descriptions and method steps keep "and".
- UK names only, from a glossary the site checks against. Coriander, spring onions, courgette, aubergine, plain flour, caster sugar, double cream, tomato purée, passata, beef mince, braising steak, baby new potatoes, stock, cornflour, bicarbonate of soda, grill, hob, frying pan, casserole, baking tray and cling film.
- UK spelling, such as chilli, yoghurt, flavour, caramelise and colour.
- Ingredient lines follow one pattern. Amount, then the ingredient, then a comma and how to prepare it, such as "2 garlic cloves, crushed".
- Method steps start with a verb, cover one stage of cooking, and run to three sentences at most. Each step gives the heat, the time and a sign that it is done, such as "for 10 minutes, until soft and golden".
- The method uses the same ingredient names as the ingredient list, word for word.
- Every recipe and plan spells out its own method in full. Rice, for example, gets its own ingredient lines and one step saying how to cook it, never a pointer to another recipe.

## 4. Changing the number of servings
Each recipe is written for its own number of servings. The site offers 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8, and opens at 2. Every ingredient has one of five scaling types, and each type has its own rounding.

**Weighed and measured amounts.** The site scales these exactly, then rounds them. Under 10g or 10ml rounds to the nearest 1. Up to 50 rounds to the nearest 5. Up to 250 rounds to the nearest 10. Up to 1,000 rounds to the nearest 25. Above 1,000 rounds to the nearest 50 and shows in kg or litres.

**Spoons.** Teaspoons round to the nearest ¼ and tablespoons to the nearest ½. Anything under 1 tbsp shows in teaspoons, and 3 tsp or more shows in tablespoons when it comes out whole. Above 6 tbsp, the amount shows in ml.

**Things you can halve**, such as onions, lemons, peppers and avocados. These round to the nearest ½.

**Things you cannot halve**, such as eggs, tortillas, sausages, chicken thighs and garlic cloves. These round to a whole number, never below 1. Anything that makes up a portion, such as sausages or chicken thighs, rounds up so nobody goes short.

**Tins and jars.** These show as whole tins when the numbers work. Otherwise the site shows the weight with the tins beside it, such as "600g chopped tomatoes (1½ x 400g tins)".

**Fixed amounts** stay the same at every size. Pinches, handfuls, a squeeze of lemon, oil for frying, and anything that comes whole, such as a chicken, a pork shoulder or a 7g sachet of yeast.

**Refinements after testing**
- At the servings a recipe was written for, every amount shows exactly as written, with no rounding.
- "About" appears only on weighed, measured and spoon amounts. Counted things, such as garlic cloves, never carry it.
- Spoon amounts switch to ml above 6 tbsp only for liquids, such as oil. Pastes such as peanut butter stay in tbsp.
- Sachets follow the tin pattern, such as "1 x 7g sachet fast-action dried yeast".

**Where rounding causes trouble, and what the site does**
- When rounding moves an amount by more than 15%, the site puts "about" in front of it.
- Baking relies on exact ratios, so recipes such as focaccia have a fixed yield, such as "Makes 1 tray". The servings control is switched off for them.
- Joints and whole birds keep their size. A recipe for 6 made for 2 tells you to cook the full amount and freeze the rest.
- Cooking times stay the same when the servings change. When you choose double the written servings or more, the site shows a line telling you to use a bigger pan and allow extra time for browning and reducing.
- When an ingredient is split across steps, such as "half the onion", each step stores its own share, so the step shows the right amount at every size.

## 5. Meal planning
- Plan leftovers forward. Rice cooked for one dinner, using 75g for each person and twice that weight of water in the Sistema microwave rice cooker, feeds Ted's rice dish the next day. A roast leaves chicken for the next day's meals, and batch cooking fills the freezer.
- Each plan says where a meal's food comes from, such as "Uses rice left from Friday's curry".
- Dishes that need cold cooked rice, such as egg fried rice, go on the day after a rice dinner, never the same day.

## 6. How the site works
- A recipe list with search and the labels as filters.
- On a phone, each recipe has Ingredients and Method tabs, with the servings control fixed at the top.
- Tap ingredients to tick them off as you gather them.
- Each method step shows its ingredients as small cards with the amounts for the servings you chose.
- Cooking mode shows one step at a time in large text, keeps the screen on, and has Next and Back buttons.
- A time in a step, such as "10 minutes", starts a timer when you tap it.
- The page remembers your servings choice for each recipe on your device.

## 7. The printout
- A4, fitted to one page where the recipe allows.
- The title in a large serif face, with the one-line description under it.
- A row of facts. Serves, preparation, cooking and oven temperature.
- Ingredients in a narrow left column with a tick box beside each, and the numbered method in the wider right column.
- Notes in a shaded box at the foot, then the source with its web address written out, and the recipe's address on the site.
- It prints at the servings you chose, in black and one accent colour, with no buttons or menus.

## 8. Keeping to the rules
Each recipe is stored as one structured data file. Before each publish, a checking script I run rejects any recipe that breaks a rule. It looks for banned units and words, an ingredient never used in the method, a step naming something missing from the list, a missing scaling type, and a missing oven temperature when a step mentions the oven.
